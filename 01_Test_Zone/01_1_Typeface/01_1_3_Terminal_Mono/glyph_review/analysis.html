CSL TERMINAL · BASE VS CSL V4, BEFORE CHUNKING

Every character both fonts draw was rendered at the same size, reduced to its centre line, and measured for stroke width along that line (stroke ends and joins excluded). The heatmaps colour each stroke by its width against that glyph's typical stroke: eventhinner (taper)thicker. In CSL V4 almost all of the red is its chunks: the deliberate thin diagonal necks. In v2 the red is JetBrains Mono's taper, which is what has to go before chunks can look like CSL's.

2 · Chunks: where CSL V4 cuts, and whether v2 is ready there

A chunk is the square where two strokes overlap at an outer corner, cut out so the strokes meet through a thin neck. v2 can take one cleanly only where that corner is one of the rebuilt CSL corner pairs (outer corner + counter corner, even stroke). Tiers are my proposal for being reserved: first = ready, simple round shapes where the cut doesn't change what the letter is; after = ready or nearly, but digits and busy shapes where a cut costs more legibility in code; base work first = the corner isn't there yet (JetBrains Mono curves, joins or diagonal tails); skip = symbols, left for their own pass.
